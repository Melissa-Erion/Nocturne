"Macros" are the three things in food that give you calories: **protein**, **carbohydrates** and **fat**. Counting them instead of just calories is what turns a diet into a plan: you lose fat but keep the muscle you're working for, and you stay full while you do it.

You need four numbers, and you can work them all out with a calculator in about five minutes. Here's how, with a real example the whole way through.

## Step 1: Estimate the calories you burn

Start with your **basal metabolic rate (BMR)**, the energy your body uses at rest. The most accurate everyday formula is **Mifflin-St Jeor**:

- **Women:** 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161
- **Men:** 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5

Then multiply by your activity level to get your **maintenance calories**, what you burn on an average day:

- Sedentary (desk job, little walking): × 1.2
- Lightly active (some walking, 1–3 workouts a week): × 1.375
- Moderately active (on your feet a fair bit, 3–5 workouts): × 1.55
- Very active (physical job or hard training most days): × 1.725

> Not sure? Pick the lower one. Most people overestimate how active they are, and it's easier to add food later than to wonder why nothing is changing.

**Example:** a 32-year-old woman, 70 kg (154 lb), 168 cm, moderately active.

- BMR = 700 + 1,050 − 160 − 161 = **1,429 kcal**
- Maintenance = 1,429 × 1.55 = **about 2,215 kcal**

## Step 2: Adjust for your goal

- **Lose fat:** eat about **15–20% below** maintenance. Regimen uses 18%.
- **Build muscle:** eat about **10% above** maintenance.
- **Recomposition** (lose fat and gain muscle slowly): about **5–10% below**.
- **Maintain:** eat at maintenance.

Bigger cuts look faster on paper but cost muscle, energy and willpower. A moderate deficit is the one you'll still be on in week eight.

**Example:** she wants to lose fat, so 2,215 × 0.82 = **about 1,816 kcal a day**.

> Don't go below your BMR, or below 1,200 kcal (women) / 1,500 kcal (men), without a professional involved. Regimen won't suggest targets under that line.

## Step 3: Set protein first

Protein is the macro that protects muscle and keeps you full, so it gets set first. Aim for **about 1 gram per pound of body weight** (2.2 g per kg). Anywhere from 0.7 g per pound up still gets most of the benefit. [Here's why that number.](/guides/how-much-protein/)

**Example:** 154 lb → **154 g of protein**. Protein has 4 calories per gram, so that's 616 kcal.

## Step 4: Set fat, then give the rest to carbs

Fat supports hormones and makes food taste good. Set it at about **25% of your calories**, and not much less than about 0.3 g per pound (0.7 g per kg). Fat has 9 calories per gram.

Carbs fuel your training. They get **whatever calories are left**, at 4 calories per gram.

**Example:**

- Fat: 1,816 × 0.25 ÷ 9 = **50 g** (450 kcal)
- Carbs: (1,816 − 616 − 450) ÷ 4 = **188 g** (750 kcal)

Her targets: **1,816 kcal · 154 g protein · 188 g carbs · 50 g fat.**

## Turning numbers into meals

Here's where most people give up: knowing you need 154 g of protein doesn't tell you what to put on the plate. Two things make it manageable:

1. **Split protein across your meals.** Four meals of about 40 g each is easier than trying to fit it all into dinner.
2. **Weigh food for the first couple of weeks.** You'll quickly learn what 150 g of chicken or 80 g of oats looks like, and then you can ease off.

Regimen does this part for you: pick the foods you like and it works out the gram amount of each so every meal lands on your numbers. [See how meal planning works.](/guides/plan-your-meals/)

## When to recalculate

Your numbers are a starting estimate, not a verdict. Weigh yourself a few mornings a week and look at the two-week trend:

- **Losing about 0.5–1% of your body weight a week?** Keep going.
- **Not moving for two weeks or more?** Drop about 100–150 kcal, mostly from carbs or fat.
- **Losing faster than 1% a week?** Add about 100–150 kcal back. Fast loss usually means lost muscle.

Regimen checks your weight trend every two weeks and suggests the adjustment for you.

## Common questions

### Do I have to hit my macros exactly?

No. Within about 5–10 g of protein and 100 kcal of calories is a great day. Consistency over weeks matters far more than any single day.

### Should rest days have different macros?

They can. Some people eat a little less (mostly fewer carbs) on rest days. It's optional: total weekly intake is what drives results. Regimen lets you set separate rest-day targets if you want them.

### Is the calculation accurate?

It's a good estimate, usually within about 10% for most people. That's why the two-week check matters: your real weight trend tells you what the formula can't.

### Do I count vegetables?

Count them if it's easy, but don't stress about a handful of spinach. Starchy ones like potatoes and corn are worth counting.

> This is general information, not medical advice. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, or have a history of disordered eating, talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before changing how you eat.
