Getting stronger comes down to doing a little more over time: more reps, then more weight. Regimen does that bookkeeping for you and turns it into a clear **Target today** for every exercise.

## The rep range

Each exercise in your plan has a rep range, like **3 × 8–12** (three sets of 8 to 12 reps), and an **RIR** target: how many reps you leave in the tank. RIR 2 means you stop when you could have done about two more.

## Double progression, in plain words

1. Start with a weight you can lift for the bottom of the range.
2. Each session, try to add a rep or two at the same weight.
3. When **every set** reaches the **top of the range** without going past your RIR target, Regimen adds the smallest weight jump for that equipment and you start again at the bottom of the range.

So for 3 × 8–12 at 40 kg: 8, 8, 8 → 10, 9, 9 → 12, 12, 12 → next time 42.5 kg × 8, 8, 8.

## What the target can say

- **Go up**: every set hit the top of the range at the right effort. Add the smallest increment.
- **Hold**: you're within the range. Keep the weight and add a rep where you can.
- **Hold (too hard)**: you reached the top, but it felt harder than your RIR target. Repeat the weight before adding more.
- **Ease off**: you fell short of the bottom of the range two sessions in a row. Regimen drops the weight by about 5% so you can build back up.
- **Find your starting weight**: no history yet. Pick a weight you could lift for a couple more reps than the range asks.

Under the target you'll see the reason, with **Accept**, **Edit** or **Ignore** buttons. You're always in charge.

## Weight jumps

The smallest jump depends on the equipment: for example 2.5 kg for a barbell or cable, 2 kg for dumbbells and 5 kg for machines. You can change the jumps to match your gym in **Me › Settings › Training**, and switch to a simpler **linear** rule (add weight whenever every set reaches the bottom of the range) if you prefer.

> Progress isn't a straight line. Bad sleep, stress or a long day can make a session harder. Regimen only eases off after two short sessions in a row, so one bad day won't undo your progress.
